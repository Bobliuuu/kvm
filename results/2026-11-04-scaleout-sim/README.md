## How the 1T simulation works (and where it is generic)

* **Topology** (`Topology`): GPU, node (8), rack (32 nodes, 256 GPUs), datacenter (4 racks), WAN between datacenters as a shared pipe per datacenter pair
  (aggregate Gb/s, one-way latency). Bandwidth/latency per tier are assumptions listed in the class (H100-class: 450 GB/s NVLink per direction, 50 GB/s NIC per GPU). Rank numbering decides which parallel axis lands on which tier; collectives are costed
  hierarchically from the actual placement of the group (`Topology.levels`), WAN-crossing collectives share the pipe.
* **Graph** (`lower`): a layer is a list of phases (flops, tensor-parallel bytes, expert all-to-all bytes). For every stage, microbatch and pass (forward, recompute,
  backward) the lowering emits compute tasks on a compute stream, tensor-parallel reduce-scatter/all-gather on an intra-node stream, ZeRO-3 gathers/reduce-scatters and expert all-to-alls on a NIC stream, pipeline sends on their own stream, and a WAN pipe task with latency when a stage boundary crosses datacenters. Pipeline order is **not** written down: depth-first priority (older microbatch first, backward over recompute over forward) plus a window edge ("forward k waits for backward k-W") gives 1F1B (peak live activations 16 layers against 53-61 for GPipe in the check). Stage boundaries come from `partition.balance_stages`, the partitioner's own linear-partition DP.
* **Honesty about "generic":** the phases and the communication each needs mirror the partitioner's layout algebra but are re-implemented at cost-model level; the real
  partitioner cannot be run at 10^4 devices (it materialises buffer values; ring collectives are O(P^2) tasks; the CPU simulator took 49 s for 5,600 tasks before its scheduler was made incremental, now the whole 64-device suite runs in about 35 s). What links the two: shared `balance_stages`, a test that compares message volumes with the real planner's output, and the 16-64 device bitwise checks above.
* **Assumptions that matter** (all in `Hw`, `Topology`, `Par`): 60% of peak on GEMMs (989 TFLOP/s dense bf16), a 5% compute slowdown while communication runs on the same SMs, full activation recompute,
  sequence-parallel tensor parallelism, ZeRO-3 gathered once per step (whole stage kept in bf16), no overlap with the previous/next step, uniform expert routing, NIC arbitration as strict priority.
* **What it does not capture:** stragglers and jitter (symmetry reduction), congestion beyond the WAN pipe, collective-algorithm tails at 10^4 ranks, GPU clock/thermal effects, the numerics of a trillion-parameter run, and the executor's dispatch cost at these task counts (AGENTS.md: the VM has only been shown to win on small dependent work). **Whether a persistent-kernel runtime beats NCCL-based stacks at this scale is still unanswered**; what the simulation shows is that the communication a no-NCCL design needs is expressible and schedulable, and how sensitive the step is to each network parameter.

## Findings (cost model)

* The dominant sensitivity is the **WAN bandwidth at the stage boundary, scaling with DP x TP**: every replica's activations cross the same inter-datacenter pipe. With the pipeline cut across 4 datacenters, 100 Gb/s per pair is enough for 512-2048 GPUs
  (1.11-1.15x the lower bound with 8 extra microbatches in flight) but not at 8192 (2.2x); 400 Gb/s restores 1.15x. An earlier arithmetic note in `DISTRIBUTED.md` (about 83 Gb/s needed, so 100 "fits") was off by 2x: it assumed one-sequence microbatches (`dist/scaling.py` fixed; needs about 83 Gb/s *per direction* at 4096 GPUs, i.e. no headroom).
* **Latency matters through the in-flight window, not the transfer**: 1 ms to 100 ms one-way changes the step by about 4% at 400 Gb/s with 8 extra microbatches in flight (section 2), but the plain 1F1B window of `pp - stage` costs 14% (400 Gb/s) to 28% (100 Gb/s) at 20 ms unless the window is widened (activation memory +3.5 GB per 8 extra microbatches).
* Putting **DP across the WAN instead of PP is 1.6x slower** at the same bandwidth (135.8 s against 83.8 s at 4096 GPUs, 400 Gb/s).
* **Overlap**: with tensor-parallel traffic on NVLink and ZeRO gathered once per step, total communication on the critical stage is a few percent of compute, so the *exposed* part is under 2% in every dense variant and "no overlap at all" costs only 2% more. The achieved step is 1.11-1.29x the lower bound (perfect overlap + 1F1B bubble); the gap is pipeline fill with WAN latency, not exposed communication. Re-gathering ZeRO weights every microbatch exposes 6.8% and costs 5% more.
  Token-chunked comm/compute pipelining (`tp_chunks=4`, which needs the row-tile ops the partitioner lacks) would close most of the gap (1.02x). The 5% SM tax is worth 5 s of 84.
* **MoE 1T** (384 experts, top-8): expert all-to-all dominates (17-36% of the step exposed, 13-16% MFU at 1-4k GPUs) because 8 x 2 x 7168 bf16 bytes per token cross a 50 GB/s NIC against 1.2 us of expert compute per token. Cutting those bytes to 0.375x (node-limited routing, fp8 dispatch: a what-if factor, not implemented) halves the exposed part. Expert groups larger than a rack (128) leave no overlap in this model.
* **Memory**: worst stage 34.9 GB at 4096 GPUs (state 4.0, gathered bf16 weights 16.1, stored activations 10.1, workspace 0.9, buffers/context/fragmentation 3.9); every configuration reported is under 80 GB (the largest is 64.4 GB at 512 GPUs, where state per GPU is 8x higher).
* **Published numbers, only what could be verified:** Megatron-LM trained a 1T-parameter model on 3072 A100-80GB at 163 TFLOP/s per GPU (52% of peak), sequence 2048 (arXiv:2104.04473, abstract and text). The model with A100 constants and the
  same shape (TP/PP/batch from memory, unverified) gives 148-199 TFLOP/s for GEMM efficiencies 0.6-0.8, so it is within the right range and **not calibrated**. DeepSeek-V3: 671B total, 37B active, 2.788M H800 GPU-hours (arXiv:2412.19437, abstract); no comparison was made (hardware and
  schedule details were not verified). Failure rates: 419 unexpected interruptions in 54 days on 16,384 H100 for Llama 3, as reported by secondary summaries of the paper (not read directly).

## Fault tolerance and stragglers (see DISTRIBUTED.md sections 9-11)

Recovery protocol and test are described in DISTRIBUTED.md section 9; work stealing needs a *fixed-order reduction by bundle id*: summing in completion order changed the fp32 bits in the test, the fixed order did not
(`tests/dist/test_scaleout.py::Resilience`). Stealing recovers 1.3-3.5x of a straggler-bound step in the DES (section 9 below); it is only legal across DP replicas, never inside a TP or PP group.

## 8. Cross-check with a published configuration
Megatron-LM, 1T parameters, 3072 A100-80GB, sequence 2048, NVLink/NVSwitch in the node and 8 x 200 Gb/s HDR per node: reported 163 TFLOP/s per GPU = 52% of peak (arXiv:2104.04473, abstract and text).
Our model with TP8 x PP64 x DP6 (TP/PP from the model's recollection of that paper, NOT verified), batch 3072 sequences, A100 constants. Kernel efficiency is a free parameter:

| 0.6 | 113.3 | 148 | 47.4% |
| 0.7 | 96.2 | 174 | 55.8% |
| 0.8 | 84.3 | 199 | 63.7% |
This brackets 163 TFLOP/s; it shows the model is not off by a large factor in a regime with the same shape, not that it is calibrated.
